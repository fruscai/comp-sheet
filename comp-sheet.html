How this works, and when not to trust it

It does what an agent does on paper, just faster.

  1. Takes what each house nearby actually sold for.
  2. Nudges that price up or down for how it differs from yours — bigger, newer, an extra bathroom, a nicer kitchen.
  3. Adjusts for how long ago it sold, if prices have moved since.
  4. Leans hardest on the sales that needed the least nudging, because those are the most similar houses and the best guide.
  5. Averages what's left. That's the number.
This is a starting point, not an appraisal. It can't see that the house backs onto a freeway, that the photos are terrible, that there's a lien on it, or that the neighborhood turned last month. A lender will not accept it. Walk the house and use your judgment.
Garbage in, garbage out. Sales from a different school district or more than a year old will pull the number in the wrong direction. Two great comps beat six mediocre ones.

Keeping your work

Two things happen so you never lose an afternoon of typing.

  • This computer quietly remembers what you typed. Close the tab, knock the power out, come back tomorrow — it's still there. You can wipe it with "Forget it" or "Start over".
  • Save to a file writes out a copy of this whole page with your numbers tucked inside. It's an ordinary file you can keep in a folder, back up, or email to someone. They double-click it and see exactly what you saw — no install, no account, no internet. Every saved sheet is a complete working copy of this tool.
